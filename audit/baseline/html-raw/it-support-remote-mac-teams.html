The day you find out you don't control your laptops

Most remote teams discover the gap when someone quits. The Mac is three states away, and it was set up on the employee's personal Apple Account. If Find My was on, Activation Lock means that even once the Mac is back and erased, nobody can set it up again without that person's password. Getting Apple to remove the lock takes the original proof of purchase and a support request.

Device management, with zero-touch enrollment through Apple Business Manager, closes that gap before it opens. So does watching sign-ins, because on a remote team the laptop and the login are the whole office.

Zero-touch enrollment through Apple Business Manager

Zero-touch means nobody in IT handles the laptop before the employee does. Here's how it works.

1. Set up Apple Business Manager

It's free from Apple. It needs a D-U-N-S number for your business, which is also free. We link it to our device management.

2. Buy through the right channel

Order Macs from Apple or a participating authorized reseller on your business account. Once your Apple customer number or reseller ID is linked, each serial number lands in your Apple Business Manager account automatically.

3. Ship to the person

The box goes straight to the employee's home, still sealed. Nobody opens it first.

4. First boot

The Mac connects to Wi-Fi, recognizes that it belongs to your organization, enrolls in management, and applies your settings: FileVault encryption, screen lock, firewall, security software, and the apps the role needs.

5. Signed in and working

The employee signs in to Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 and enrolls in 2-step verification. From then on, patches and settings follow the laptop wherever it goes.

Macs you already own can join too. They're added to Apple Business Manager with Apple Configurator, which means erasing and setting them up again, and after that they enroll like new ones. For the first 30 days after a Mac is added this way, the user can remove it from management. For more on the setup, see our zero-touch Mac deployment guide.

What device management can and can't see

Good employees ask this first, and they deserve a straight answer. On a company-owned Mac, device management is a configuration tool, not a surveillance tool. It's also only one of the tools on a managed laptop, so the full picture is below.

What it can see

The Mac's model, serial number, and macOS version. Storage capacity and whether encryption is on. The list of installed apps. Whether required security settings are in place.

What it can do

Push settings and Wi-Fi profiles, install and update apps, require FileVault encryption and a screen lock, and lock or erase the Mac if it's lost or its owner leaves.

What it doesn't see

Device management doesn't read files, email, messages, browser history, or photos, and it doesn't track location.

Other tools on a managed laptop see more, and we'd rather you hear it from us:

  • Monitoring agent. Reports the laptop's health and patch status and lets us run maintenance and fixes remotely.
  • Remote support. Lets us see the screen to fix a problem during a support session.
  • Managed detection and response. Watches for attacker behavior, like an unknown program making changes it shouldn't or a burst of files being encrypted, and flags it for a security analyst.
  • DNS filtering. Sees which websites the laptop looks up, so it can block malicious ones.
  • Your work account. Your employer, as the Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 admin, can access work email and files. That's true with or without us.

We use these tools to keep laptops working and to catch attackers, not to review anyone's work.

Personal devices are a separate conversation. People on their own hardware can be covered through their accounts, with 2-step verification and identity monitoring, instead of device management.

Sign-ins from more than one country

When part of your team works from another country, blocking foreign sign-ins isn't an option. Their normal is someone else's red flag.

So we monitor instead of block. Identity monitoring flags sign-ins that don't fit each person's pattern, like a login from a third country, or two sign-ins too far apart to travel between. 2-step verification is enforced on every account without text-message codes, which can be hijacked through a SIM swap and are unreliable across borders anyway. Staff who travel can tell us before they go, so a real login from an airport doesn't get treated like an attack.

Laptop lifecycle for staff you never meet

Purchase

We spec the Mac for the role, you buy it through a channel that registers it to your Apple Business Manager account, and we coordinate delivery to the person's door. For staff outside the US, we work out with you whether buying in-country or shipping from the US makes more sense.

Ship

It arrives at the employee's door, sealed. It configures itself on first boot, so there's nothing to install over a video call.

Wipe

When someone leaves, their account is suspended and the Mac is locked or erased remotely in the same offboarding step, wherever it is, once it's online.

Recover

We coordinate getting the laptop back. Once it's erased, it re-enrolls itself for the next hire. If it never comes back, it stays assigned to your organization in Apple Business Manager, so after a reset it re-enrolls when it's set up online, and it can be locked the next time it connects to the internet.

Onboarding checklist for a new remote hire

The same steps every time, whether the new hire is in Oakland or overseas.

Before day one

Create the work account and add it to the right groups and shared drives. Order the Mac from Apple or a participating reseller on your business account, so it lands in Apple Business Manager, and ship it to their home. On Secure Growth and up, put any shared logins they'll need in the password vault.

Day one

They unbox the Mac, connect to Wi-Fi, and it enrolls itself. They sign in and enroll in 2-step verification. We confirm the laptop reports as encrypted, patched, and protected.

First week

Security awareness training assigned. Access checked against what the role actually needs. On Secure Growth and up, their mail and files confirmed in backup.

When they leave

Account suspended and signed out everywhere. Files transferred to their manager. Shared passwords they knew rotated. The Mac locked or erased remotely.

Remote Mac team IT: common questions

Can my company see what I do on my work Mac?
Device management on its own doesn't read your files, email, messages, or browser history, and it doesn't track location. It sees the Mac's model, macOS version, installed apps, and whether security settings are on. Other tools on a managed laptop see more: the monitoring agent reports health and patch status and lets us run maintenance remotely, our remote support tool can see your screen during a support session, the security agent watches for attacker behavior, and DNS filtering sees which sites are looked up so it can block malicious ones. And your employer, as the admin of your work account, can access work email and files.
How do you set up a Mac for a remote employee you've never met?
Through Apple Business Manager. You buy the Mac on your business account, it ships sealed to the employee's home, and on first boot it enrolls itself in device management and applies your settings and apps. Nobody in IT has to touch it.
What happens to the laptop when a remote employee quits?
Their account is suspended and the Mac is locked or erased remotely in the same offboarding step. Because it's enrolled through Apple Business Manager, we can prevent or clear Activation Lock, so their personal Apple Account can't strand it. Once it's back and erased, it re-enrolls itself for the next hire.
Can you support employees in other countries?
Yes. Identity monitoring and 2-step verification work wherever someone signs in, and device management works anywhere the Mac has internet. To be straight about hours: our help desk runs 8am to 6pm Pacific, Monday through Friday, which is evening to early morning for staff in India. Critical incidents carry our 4-hour notification commitment.
How much does managed IT cost for a remote team on Macs?
$125 to $250 per person per month, plus $15 per Mac for device management. A 10-person team on Secure Growth ($175 per person) is 10 × $175 + 10 × $15 = $1,900 a month. Teams of 1 to 4 pay $600 flat, plus $15 per Mac. Apple fleet onboarding is scoped and quoted separately.
Do we need Apple Business Manager?
For zero-touch setup, yes. It's free, and it needs a free D-U-N-S number for your business. Macs outside Apple Business Manager can still be managed, with more manual steps and less control.
