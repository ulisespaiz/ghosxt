How Often Should a Small Business Replace Computers? (2026)

TL;DR: Business laptops and desktops are worth keeping for 4 to 5 years. Past that, repairs, slowdowns, and missing security updates cost more than a new machine. Replace a quarter of your fleet each year instead of everything at once.

Three steps for this week.

  1. List every computer with its age. Note purchase date, user, and whether it can run a supported Windows or macOS version.
  2. Mark anything over 4 years old. Those are your replacement candidates, oldest and most sensitive first.
  3. Put one quarter of the cost in next year's budget. Steady replacement beats an emergency purchase.

Small businesses often run a computer until it dies. It feels thrifty, and it is not. A five-year-old laptop that takes four minutes to open a spreadsheet costs you real money in lost time, and one that can no longer get security updates puts the whole business at risk.

This guide covers how long business computers should last, the signs it is time to replace one, and how to spread the cost so it never becomes a surprise.

1. How long a business computer should last

The honest answer is 4 to 5 years for most roles. Hardware rarely fails on a schedule, but performance, warranty coverage, and software support all fade together.

  • Laptops: about 4 years. Batteries wear down, hinges and ports take daily abuse, and the machines travel. Expect the battery to need replacing around year 3.
  • Desktops: 5 years, sometimes 6. They run cooler and stay put. A desktop with an SSD and enough memory can serve a front-office role well past year 5.
  • Warranty is a useful marker. Most business-grade machines ship with a 3-year warranty. Once it lapses, every repair is your bill.
  • Operating system support sets a hard stop. When the maker ends updates, the device is done, whatever its age. Our guide to Windows 10 end of life shows what that looks like in practice.

2. Signs it is time to replace

Age is a starting point. These signs tell you a specific machine has run its course.

  • It cannot run a supported operating system. This is the one that cannot wait. Without updates, new flaws stay open. See how this fits into patch management.
  • Staff are working around it. Slow startup, freezing during video calls, and apps that crash are lost hours multiplied across every day.
  • Repairs keep coming. If repairs in a year approach a third of the price of a new machine, stop repairing.
  • It cannot take modern security features. Older hardware may lack the chip support needed for disk encryption and current endpoint protection.
  • Nobody can say who uses it. A machine with no owner and no record is both a cost and a risk. An asset inventory fixes that.

3. How to plan a refresh without a budget shock

The goal is a steady, predictable cost with no emergencies.

  • Stagger it. Replace roughly a quarter of the fleet each year. You never face one big invoice, and you always have a few newer spares.
  • Buy business-grade, not bargain-grade. Consumer models cost less up front but ship with shorter warranties and weaker management features.
  • Standardize. Fewer models mean faster setup, easier support, and interchangeable spare parts. Pair it with device management so a new laptop arrives configured.
  • Retire old machines properly. Remove them from your accounts and wipe or destroy the drive. Follow our checklist for secure disposal of old computers.
  • Put it in the plan. Hardware belongs in your annual IT budget, not in the emergency column.

The takeaway

A computer is a tool with a working life. Plan for 4 to 5 years, watch for the signs it is done, and replace a slice of the fleet every year. You trade a few predictable expenses for fewer outages, faster staff, and no machines sitting unpatched in a corner. Frequently asked questions

How often should a small business replace its computers?

Plan on 4 to 5 years for business laptops and desktops. Laptops carry more wear and often hit the limit closer to 4 years, while a well-specified desktop can reach 5 or 6. Replace sooner if the device can no longer run a supported operating system or cannot be patched.

Is it cheaper to repair an old computer or replace it?

Repair makes sense for a single failed part on a machine under about 3 years old. Once a computer is past 4 years, repeated repairs, lost staff time, and security gaps usually cost more than a replacement. A simple rule: if repairs in a year approach a third of the replacement price, replace it.

What happens if I keep using computers that cannot run a supported Windows version?

They stop receiving security updates, so every new flaw stays open for good. Cyber insurers and compliance frameworks can also treat unsupported systems as a finding. Replace the device or retire it from anything that touches business data.

Should I replace all computers at once or in batches?

In batches. Replacing about a quarter of your fleet each year smooths the cost, avoids a single large expense, and keeps spare machines on hand. Put the oldest devices and the ones used for sensitive work first.

What should I do with the old computers?

Back up what is needed, remove the device from your management and accounts, and wipe the drive or physically destroy it before it leaves your hands. Do not donate or recycle a computer with data still on it.
