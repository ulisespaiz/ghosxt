Microsoft 365 vs Google Workspace for a 10-Person Business

Ulises Paiz, Owner, Ghosxt

Ulises Paiz is the owner and sole engineer at Ghosxt, a Salinas, CA managed IT and cybersecurity provider. He holds an M.S. in Cybersecurity and Information Assurance (WGU, 2026) and nine industry certifications including CompTIA SecurityX, CySA+, and Microsoft AZ-104, with prior DoD and federal contractor infrastructure experience. More about Ulises →

TL;DR: There's no universal winner. The right platform matches how your team already works, not a marketing checklist.

Short answer for a 10-person business:

  1. Already living in Gmail, Docs, and mobile devices? Google Workspace is usually the simpler, lower-friction path.
  2. Depend on desktop Excel, Outlook scheduling, or Windows-only software? Microsoft 365 is usually the better fit.
  3. Handle controlled unclassified information as a defense contractor? Look at Microsoft 365 GCC High, not either platform's commercial tier.
  4. Either way, the security controls that matter, enforced MFA, device management, and real backup, exist on both platforms and neither ships turned all the way on.

The decision is about workflow fit, device fleet, and compliance need, not which platform is "more secure" out of the box.

We get asked this question almost every time a new business calls, and the honest answer disappoints people looking for a winner: both Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace can run a 10-person business securely and reliably. The platforms differ in how they think about identity, devices, and files, and those differences matter more than any feature checklist. A team that already lives in Gmail and Google Docs loses very little by staying there. A team built around Excel models, Outlook calendars, and Windows-specific software loses real productivity by switching away from Microsoft. Pick based on how your team already works and what you're required to prove to a customer or regulator, not based on which vendor's sales page sounds more secure.

Identity and MFA: Conditional Access vs. context-aware access

Both platforms let you require more than a password, but they approach it differently. Microsoft Entra Conditional Access is an if-then policy engine: if a user or an agent wants to access a resource under certain conditions (an unmanaged device, an unusual location, a risky sign-in), then a specific action is required, multifactor authentication, a compliant device, or a block. It's flexible and layered on top of the identity signals Microsoft already collects, and it's the piece Microsoft 365 hardening is built around; see the official Conditional Access overview.

Google Workspace's equivalent is Context-Aware Access, which restricts access to apps and data based on a user's identity, device, and location. Alongside it, the highest-impact single setting on Google Workspace is enforced 2-Step Verification for every user, not just the option to turn it on. Both platforms support phishing-resistant methods, security keys and passkeys on Google Workspace, and phishing-resistant authentication methods within Conditional Access on Microsoft 365, which stop the credential-phishing pattern behind most account takeovers far more reliably than a one-time code sent by text. If you're already on Google Workspace, our Google Workspace security settings guide covers the full rollout.

Device management: Intune vs. Google endpoint management, and where Macs fit

A 10-person business rarely runs one device type, and this is where the platforms diverge most. Microsoft Intune is a mature, single policy engine for Windows, iOS, Android, and macOS, and it pairs with Apple Business Manager for automated device enrollment, commonly called zero-touch enrollment: a new Mac or iPhone ships to an employee, powers on, and enrolls itself into management and your security baseline without anyone touching a configuration profile by hand. Microsoft documents this pairing in its automated device enrollment setup for iOS/iPadOS guidance, and the same enrollment token pattern extends to Mac fleets.

Google Workspace's endpoint management covers mobile devices (basic and advanced tiers) and extends to computers, including Context-Aware Access checks on managed machines. It's a real, supported path, not an afterthought, but Mac-heavy fleets on Google Workspace commonly layer Apple Business Manager's zero-touch enrollment underneath it for provisioning, since Google's own device management console is built mobile-first. If your business is Mac-first and remote by default, our managed IT for fully remote, Mac-first teams page walks through how that pairing works in practice.

Email, files, and the desktop Office app problem

Exchange Online and Outlook versus Gmail on one side, SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams versus Drive, Docs, and Meet on the other: both are genuinely capable, cloud-native systems now, not the "real product vs. web toy" gap that existed a decade ago. The deciding factor is usually desktop application dependence. A business running complex Excel models with macros, add-ins tied to Windows-only accounting or industry software, or heavy Outlook calendar and delegate-access workflows will feel real friction moving off Microsoft 365, because the desktop Office apps and their ecosystem of plug-ins are still Windows-first. A business that lives in a browser, collaborates in real time on shared documents, and doesn't depend on desktop-only software has very little to gain from Microsoft's heavier desktop footprint. Our OneDrive vs. SharePoint vs. Teams guide is useful if you're already on Microsoft 365 and unsure where a file belongs.

Compliance: defense contractors and controlled unclassified information

If your business handles controlled unclassified information (CUI) as a defense contractor, or is subject to International Traffic in Arms Regulations, standard commercial Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace isn't the right tenant, full stop. Microsoft's GCC High and DoD service description lays out what changes: a separate, vetted environment assessed against NIST SP 800-53 controls at a FIPS 199 High categorization, with background-checked support staff and eligibility requirements you go through before an environment is provisioned. There isn't a direct Google Workspace equivalent commonly used in this exact niche, which is one reason Microsoft 365 GCC High tends to be the default answer for defense contractors, independent of which platform the rest of the business prefers. If CMMC and NIST 800-171 are on your radar at all, read our CMMC compliance page first; the compliance assessment itself runs through a third-party assessor, not through your IT provider.

Licensing tiers, at a high level

Both vendors organize small business plans the same general way: a small-business family capped at 300 users, and an enterprise family above that with no user cap. Microsoft's small-business family is Business Basic, Business Standard, and Business Premium, documented in its official plan options service description. Google's is Business Starter, Business Standard, and Business Plus, with Enterprise tiers above that, detailed on Google Workspace's current pricing page. A 10-person business fits comfortably inside either family's entry tiers. Plan names, included storage, and prices change often enough on both sides that we won't quote a number here; check each vendor's page linked above for what's current before you buy.

Backup: neither platform's retention is a backup

This is the single most common misunderstanding on both platforms. Microsoft 365's recycle bins and version history, and Google Workspace's trash window and Vault retention, all exist to undo accidents and satisfy legal hold, not to restore your business after a compromised account is purged, a departing employee deletes a shared drive, or a sync client propagates ransomware-encrypted files across every connected device. Both gaps are closed the same way: a real, independent backup layered on top of the platform, not a setting you flip inside it.

Switching cost

Moving between platforms is rarely just a mailbox migration. Budget for retraining on a genuinely different interface, rebuilding any macros or add-ins tied to desktop Office apps, re-mapping SharePoint or Drive sharing permissions rather than assuming they'll translate automatically, and a mail-flow cutover window where deliverability needs active monitoring. For a 10-person business this is usually a days-to-weeks project, not a same-day switch, and it's a reason to get the initial platform choice right rather than plan on switching later.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for a small business, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace?

Neither is universally better. If your team already works in Gmail, Docs, and mobile devices, and doesn't depend on desktop-only Windows software, Google Workspace is usually the simpler path. If your team relies on desktop Excel, Outlook-based scheduling, or Windows-specific line-of-business software, or you're a defense contractor that handles controlled unclassified information, Microsoft 365 is usually the better fit. Both platforms can be secured properly with the right settings turned on.

Is Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 cheaper for 10 users?

It depends on which tier of each platform you need, and both vendors change plan names and pricing periodically. Rather than quote a number that may be out of date, check each vendor's current published pricing directly: Microsoft's Business plans and Google's Business plans are both capped at 300 users, so a 10-person business fits comfortably in either family's entry tiers.

Does either platform back up my email and files automatically?

No. Both platforms include recovery windows, version history, and retention tools, but none of that is an independent, point-in-time backup with its own restore path. A compromised account, a malicious insider, or a sync client that propagates ransomware-encrypted files can outrun those windows on either platform. A real backup for Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace is a separate control layered on top.

Can Macs be managed properly on both platforms?

Yes, though the maturity differs. Microsoft Intune plus Apple Business Manager gives Macs zero-touch enrollment and the same policy engine used for Windows and mobile devices. Google Workspace's endpoint management covers computers alongside mobile devices, but a Mac-heavy fleet on Google Workspace often benefits from the same Apple Business Manager zero-touch enrollment layered in for device provisioning.

What should a defense contractor use instead of standard Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace?

A defense contractor that handles controlled unclassified information (CUI) should look at Microsoft 365 GCC High rather than commercial Microsoft 365 or standard Google Workspace. Standard commercial cloud tenants aren't built for CUI handling. The compliance assessment itself is arranged through a third-party assessor; Ghosxt handles the GCC High setup and hardening, not the assessment or CMMC documentation.
