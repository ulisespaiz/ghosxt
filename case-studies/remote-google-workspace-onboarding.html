A five-person professional-services firm, fully remote, running Google Workspace on Macs, with team members in the US and India, was fully onboarded onto our Secure Growth plan in about two weeks. At published prices, Secure Growth ($175 per user) comes to $875 a month for five people. Below: the situation, what was at risk, what we did and in what order, the outcome, and what a team this size pays.

The situation

The firm is small and fully remote, built on Google Workspace and Macs, with part of the team in the US and part in India. It had compliance requirements to meet and went looking for a managed IT provider.

They found us by describing those compliance requirements to an AI search tool and adding "MSP." Ghosxt came up. What decided it: they could talk directly to the owner.

What was at risk

A remote firm on Google Workspace has no office to lock. The accounts and the laptops are the office, so that's where the exposure sits:

  • Accounts. The firm's work lives in Gmail and Drive, behind a login. One phished password, from anywhere in the world, can open all of it.
  • Sign-ins from two countries. With staff in the US and India, "unusual location" can't simply mean "foreign." Without monitoring, a stolen login from a third country looks like one more sign-in.
  • Text-message codes. 2-step verification by SMS can be hijacked through a SIM swap and is unreliable across borders.
  • Laptops. Macs in staff homes need a central way to be encrypted, patched, locked, and erased.
  • Data. Google's retention isn't a backup. A deleted account or a bad sync can take files with it.

What we did, in order

Onboarding took about two weeks. We worked identity first, because that's where a remote firm's risk concentrates, then devices, then people.

1. 2-step verification, enforced tenant-wide

Required for every account in the firm's Google Workspace, with text-message codes turned off.

2. Identity monitoring on Workspace

Every account's sign-ins watched, with alerts on activity that doesn't fit the team's normal pattern across the US and India.

3. Gmail and Drive backup

A separate, restorable copy of the firm's mail and files, independent of Google's own retention.

4. Apple Business Manager and device management

The firm's Macs enrolled in device management through Apple Business Manager, so they can be encrypted, patched, locked, or erased from anywhere.

5. A laptop shipped pre-configured

One company-owned laptop was shipped to a remote contractor already configured.

6. Security awareness training

The team enrolled in security awareness training on spotting and reporting phishing email.

The outcome

About two weeks after kickoff, the firm had:

  • 2-step verification required on every account, with no text-message codes
  • Identity monitoring on every Google Workspace account
  • Gmail and Drive backed up to a separate, restorable copy, independent of Google's own retention
  • Macs enrolled through Apple Business Manager and under device management
  • A remote contractor working on a company-owned laptop that arrived configured
  • Security awareness training in place for the team
  • One person to call: the owner

We aren't going to put numbers on this that we can't show. What changed is that the firm's protections are enforced, monitored, and recoverable, with one accountable person behind them.
