Why Monterey contractors are under pressure right now

Most of the pressure does not arrive from the Department of Defense directly. It arrives from the prime. A subcontract comes up for renewal and the flow-down language is longer than last year. A buyer emails a supplier security questionnaire with a two-week turnaround. Someone in contracts asks for your SPRS score and nobody is sure one has ever been submitted. On the Peninsula that lands on small teams with no internal IT, where the person answering the questionnaire also runs payroll.

The second pattern is timing. Cyber requirements increasingly show up mid-contract rather than at award. A modification adds a clause. A prime updates its supplier standard and gives ninety days. Work that would have been comfortable across a year has to happen while the contract is running. That is why scoping matters more than tooling: the smaller the boundary that touches controlled data, the less there is to fix under someone else’s deadline.

Geography adds a wrinkle. Defense work in Monterey County clusters around research, training, language, engineering, and technical services rather than large production plants, so the typical contractor here is small and mobile. Laptops travel. People work from a home office in Marina on Tuesday and a client site in Seaside on Thursday. A control set that assumes one firewall in one building will not survive contact with how these teams operate.

What CMMC-ready IT looks like day to day

Certification language is abstract. The environment underneath it is not. These are the six things that change in the systems your team touches every morning.

Identity & MFA

Phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication on every account, legacy authentication switched off, and Conditional Access deciding which devices and locations reach contract data. Identity is where most small-contractor incidents start and where the cheapest wins live.

Endpoint Security & Device Management

Encrypted, patched, enrolled laptops with EDR on all of them, managed centrally so a machine that leaves the company can be wiped the same day. Personal devices stop touching contract data by accident.

Logging & Monitoring

Sign-in, file access, and endpoint telemetry collected, retained, and alerted on, with a human at the end of the alert. Audit requirements are not satisfied by logs nobody can produce nine months later.

Access Control & Least Privilege

Access defined by what a role genuinely needs, standing admin rights removed, separate accounts for administrative work, and a documented review so permissions stop accumulating as people move between projects.

Backups & Recovery

Backups the production environment cannot reach or delete, with restores tested rather than assumed. Recovery and media protection are part of the control set, not an IT nicety bolted on later. See backup & DR.

Microsoft 365 & GCC High

Tenant configuration built around where your data is permitted to live: Microsoft 365 GCC High when a contract requires it, hardened commercial Microsoft 365 when it does not. See cloud & Microsoft 365 in Monterey.

How this relates to NIST 800-171

Every item above maps back to a control family in NIST SP 800-171, the publication CMMC Level 2 is built on. Access control, identification and authentication, audit and accountability, configuration management, media protection, system and communications protection: the family names are dry, but the implementations are the ordinary parts of a well-run network done deliberately and written down where somebody else can check them.

The methodology side lives on a different page. The gap assessment across all 110 controls, CUI enclave scoping, the System Security Plan, the Plan of Action and Milestones, and the SPRS score and its submission are covered in detail on our CMMC compliance and NIST 800-171 page. Read that one for how the readiness program is run. This page is about the local engagement on the Peninsula and the systems work that sits underneath it.

What we do, and what a C3PAO does

It is worth being precise about roles, because the market is not always precise. Ghosxt is a managed IT and security provider. We prepare your environment: we scope it, implement the controls, configure Microsoft 365, produce the evidence, and keep it maintained as the business changes. We are not an assessor, and we do not issue certifications of any kind.

The formal Level 2 certification is performed by an accredited C3PAO, and a credible provider should not both prepare you and certify you. We also never guarantee a certification outcome, and you should treat any provider who does as a warning sign rather than a bargain. What preparation actually buys is that the assessment confirms what is already true about your systems instead of discovering it in front of you.

The same discipline applies long before an assessment is on the calendar. When a prime sends a supplier security questionnaire, we help you answer it accurately, which sometimes means recording that a control is partially implemented with a remediation date attached. Overstating your posture to a prime is a worse problem than having a gap, and much harder to walk back once it is in their supplier file.

FAQs about CMMC and IT support in Monterey

Do you perform the official CMMC assessment?
No. Ghosxt prepares you. The formal Level 2 certification is performed by an accredited C3PAO, and a credible provider should not both prepare you and certify you. We do not guarantee a certification outcome, and you should treat any provider who does as a warning sign. What we handle is the readiness work: scoping, control implementation, configuration, evidence, and documentation, so the assessment confirms what is already true about your systems.
We are a small subcontractor on the Peninsula with one DoD contract. Does CMMC apply to us?
Probably. The trigger is not headcount, it is what your systems touch. If your contract or purchase order carries DFARS 252.204-7012, or the prime is sending you drawings, specifications, or data marked as controlled, the requirement follows the data down to you. The first step for a small Peninsula sub is scoping: who handles that data, on which devices, and whether that footprint can shrink before you spend anything on tooling.
Can you set up Microsoft 365 GCC High for a Monterey contractor?
Yes, and we will also tell you when you do not need it. GCC High is the right answer when a contract or a prime specifies it, or when export-controlled technical data is in play. Plenty of Monterey contractors handling federal contract information are better served by hardened commercial Microsoft 365, at a fraction of the licensing and migration cost. We scope that against your contract language before anyone buys a license.
A prime just asked for our SPRS score. What happens next?
The score comes from a NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment of the environment that handles the data, so the work is to assess honestly, score it, and submit. A low number is normal for a company that has never done this, and a Plan of Action and Milestones with real dates answers a prime better than a score nobody can defend. The methodology is on our CMMC compliance page.
Do you handle everyday IT support too, or only the compliance work?
Both, and we prefer both. Compliance that sits outside your normal IT drifts within a quarter, because users get added and laptops get replaced without anyone updating the documentation. Ghosxt runs help desk, patching, monitoring, backup, and vendor coordination for Monterey contractors, with CMMC control maintenance and evidence collection folded into the same service.
