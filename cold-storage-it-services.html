What we do for cold storage operations

A refrigerated warehouse is a hard building to run a network in. Steel racking blocks signal, condensation ruins hardware never rated for it, and there is no overnight window where nothing happens. Here is the work.

Warehouse and freezer Wi-Fi that covers the racking

Coverage in a cold building is an RF problem before it is an IT problem. Metal panel walls, loaded racking, ice, and moving pallets all work against you. We survey the actual space, place access points for the aisles rather than the ceiling grid, and specify antennas and enclosures rated for cooler and freezer conditions.

Learn more

Scanners, tablets, and mobile devices

Handhelds are the operation. We handle provisioning, mobile device management, roaming so a device does not drop its session crossing from dock to freezer, spare pool and battery planning, and a repeatable rebuild so a dead scanner is replaced in minutes.

Learn more

Switching, VLANs, and segmentation

Office, warehouse operations, monitoring, and cameras and sensors each belong on their own segment with explicit rules between them. A sensor gateway is not something you want sharing a network with accounting, and the reverse holds too. Managed switching, documented ports, no mystery cables.

Learn more

WAN redundancy and failover

Temperature monitoring and alarm callouts are only as reliable as the connection they ride on. We build dual ISP paths with automatic failover, LTE backup where a second wired circuit is impractical, and monitoring that proves the backup works before the night you need it.

Learn more

Vendor and remote access control

Monitoring vendors, WMS vendors, door and access-control installers, and refrigeration contractors all ask for remote access eventually. Each gets a named, scoped, logged account rather than a shared credential, switched off between service visits.

Cybersecurity, backups, and disaster recovery for 24/7 operations

There is no quiet Sunday to restore into. We run EDR, MFA, and monitored backups with tested restores, plan maintenance around the narrowest window you have, and set a recovery order that puts receiving and shipping back first.

The connectivity behind cold-chain monitoring

Temperature monitoring platforms belong to their vendors, and so do the probes, the gateways, and the alarm rules. Ghosxt does not operate those platforms. What we own is everything an alert has to cross before it reaches a person.

The network path an alert depends on

A sensor gateway that cannot reach the internet is a sensor monitoring nothing. We keep the switch ports, VLANs, DNS, and firewall rules for monitoring hardware documented and stable, so a routine change elsewhere does not quietly cut the path an alarm needs.

Power protection for the network gear

Facility power is usually well handled for refrigeration and poorly handled for the closet holding the switch, the firewall, and the modem. We put network gear on a UPS with monitored batteries and a controlled shutdown path. A generator that carries the compressors but drops the switch still leaves an alarm nowhere to go.

Failover paths that have actually been exercised

A second circuit or LTE backup only helps if someone has proved it works. We fail the primary path on a schedule, confirm the secondary carries the traffic that matters, and document that state so nobody discovers it for the first time at 2am.

Making sure a page reaches a person

Alert delivery fails in quiet ways: a former employee's number still on the escalation list, a distribution group nobody reads, a mail rule filing alarms into a folder. We keep the identity, contact, and mail-flow side current, and test that the message lands with someone awake and authorized to act.

Refrigeration stays with your refrigeration contractor and the monitoring platform stays with its vendor. Our responsibility is the network, the power protection behind it, the failover, and the identity plumbing that turns a sensor reading into a phone call somebody answers.

Common IT problems we see at cold storage facilities

Four patterns we run into repeatedly in refrigerated buildings. Details are generalized, but anyone who has managed a cooler or freezer warehouse will recognize them.

Scanner dead zones in the far aisles

Picks slow down in the back half of the building and nobody opens a ticket, because the workaround is walking toward the dock until the device syncs. The cause is usually access points placed for an empty building, before the racking went in. We resurvey with racking loaded, reposition access points for aisle coverage, and tune power and channels so devices roam instead of clinging to a distant radio.

An alarm callout that failed silently because the only line was down

A single ISP circuit drops overnight. Refrigeration is fine and the sensors are fine, but the platform has no path to send anything through, so nobody finds out until morning. The fix is a second path with automatic failover plus a heartbeat confirming the monitoring gateway is still reachable, so silence gets treated as a symptom rather than good news.

A flat network where an office PC can reach door controllers and cameras

Cameras, door controllers, sensor gateways, and label printers get installed by whoever was on site that week, and they land on the same network as the office. Those devices rarely get firmware updates and often ship with default credentials in place. We inventory what is genuinely on the network, move each class onto its own segment, and put management interfaces behind controlled access.

Seasonal surge staffing on shared logins

Volume spikes and the building fills with temporary crews. Shared warehouse and scanner logins get passed around because provisioning individual accounts takes too long during a rush. Then nobody can tell who did what, and the accounts stay live long after the season ends. We build a staffing template with pre-staged accounts tied to role, scripted deactivation on exit, and shared-device sign-in that still identifies the person.

FAQs about IT support for cold storage facilities

Do you service refrigeration equipment?
No. We support the IT and network systems around it. Compressors, evaporators, refrigerant work, and equipment controls stay with your refrigeration contractor. What we handle is the network your monitoring gateways and alarm systems ride on, power protection for that gear, failover paths, and the contact lists that decide whether an alert reaches a person.
Can you fix Wi-Fi dead zones in a freezer or cooler?
Usually, yes. Cold rooms and high-bay racking are difficult for wireless, but they are not unpredictable. We survey the building loaded rather than empty, place access points down the aisles, use antennas and enclosures rated for the temperature and condensation, and tune roaming so a scanner hands off cleanly. Coverage gets verified with a walk test at pick height.
We run 24/7. When do you do maintenance?
We plan around your inbound and outbound schedules and the gaps between them, and build changes so they can be backed out inside the same window. For work that cannot happen live, we stage and test in parallel, then cut over at the quietest point in your week.
Do you work with our WMS or temperature monitoring vendor?
Yes. We do not resell warehouse management or monitoring platforms and we do not take over the vendor's application support. We run the infrastructure they depend on, manage accounts and access, keep scanning workflows healthy, and give vendor support teams controlled remote access when they need it.
What does cybersecurity look like for a facility full of sensors and cameras?
Segmentation first. Cameras, sensor gateways, door controllers, and printers go on their own networks with explicit rules about what they can reach and what can reach them. Then inventory, removing default credentials, tracking firmware, and putting management interfaces behind controlled access. On the staff side: MFA, EDR, named accounts, and tested backups.
