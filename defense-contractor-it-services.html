Frameworks and requirements we build toward

We are not attorneys, auditors, or assessors. We are the people who make the technical half true. Below is what small defense suppliers build toward and what the IT side of each looks like. Where a requirement becomes a legal question, we work alongside your contracts staff or compliance advisor.

NIST SP 800-171

The 110 security requirements covering controlled unclassified information in nonfederal systems, grouped into families such as access control, identification and authentication, audit and accountability, configuration management, and media protection. We implement the technical controls, capture the evidence behind each, and keep an honest record of what is still partial.

CMMC 2.0 levels

Level 1 covers basic safeguarding of federal contract information. Level 2 aligns to the full NIST SP 800-171 control set for CUI, verified by self-assessment or an accredited third party depending on the contract. Level 3 raises the bar again for the highest-priority programs. Establishing which level your contracts require comes before any spending, because building to the wrong one is expensive in both directions.

DFARS clauses arriving in contracts

DFARS 252.204-7012 is the clause most small subs meet first: safeguarding covered defense information and reporting cyber incidents on a clock. Related assessment and CMMC clauses follow it into awards and modifications. When new language lands mid-contract, we translate it into technical changes with owners and dates.

ITAR and EAR data handling

Deciding what is export controlled, and who may access it, is your export compliance responsibility rather than ours. Our part is making that policy enforceable: identity-based restrictions on who can open technical data, storage and tenant choices that keep it in permitted locations, sharing controls, and a usable access trail.

None of this is legal advice, and none of it is a certification. Ghosxt prepares environments and produces evidence. The formal Level 2 certification is performed by an accredited C3PAO, and a credible provider should not both prepare you and certify you. We do not guarantee outcomes, and we say plainly which controls are implemented and which are not.

Common IT problems we see at small defense subs

Four patterns that repeat across small defense and government suppliers. None require a sophisticated attacker to become a contract problem.

S

A prime asks for your SPRS score

The request comes from a buyer, not a contracting officer, and it wants a number by Friday. The company has never run a NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment, so there is no score and nothing behind one. The answer is not to invent a figure. Assess the environment that handles the data, score it honestly, submit it, and attach a dated plan to the gaps. Primes handle low scores constantly. Silence they cannot work with.

S

CUI sitting in a personal OneDrive

An engineer needed a drawing at home on a Sunday, dropped it into a personal cloud account, and the copy stayed there. The company had never given people a sanctioned way to work off-site, so a shortcut became the process. We close it from both ends: block the unsanctioned paths through tenant and endpoint policy, and stand up a supported location for the same work.

S

A shared admin password on the CAD workstation

One local account, one password everyone knows, on a sticky note or in a group chat, on the machine that holds the design files. It is the fastest way to lose the audit trail, because every action belongs to nobody. We replace it with named accounts, remove standing local administrator rights, and rotate what was shared.

S

A subcontract paused pending questionnaire answers

The award is ready and the security questionnaire is not. Someone is guessing at answers about encryption, logging, and incident response, and every guess is a statement to a customer. We work it item by item, mark what is genuinely in place, remediate what fits the deadline, and write a dated plan for the rest. An accurate answer clears faster than an optimistic one that unravels later.

FAQs about IT services for defense contractors

Do you certify our company for CMMC?
No. Ghosxt is a managed IT and security provider, not an assessor, and we do not issue certifications. The formal Level 2 certification is performed by an accredited C3PAO, and a credible provider should not both prepare you and certify you. We never guarantee a certification outcome. Our work is technical readiness: scoping, control implementation, configuration, evidence, and documentation.
We have one federal subcontract and no internal IT. Where do we start?
With scope, not shopping. The first question is which systems and which people actually touch federal contract information or controlled unclassified information, because everything downstream is priced off that number. Small suppliers routinely find the real footprint is four laptops and one SharePoint site. From there we sequence: identity and MFA first, then endpoints and Microsoft 365, then logging and backups.
Can you work alongside our compliance consultant or contracts staff?
Yes, and that split usually works well. Consultants and contracts professionals interpret the clause language and decide what the contract obligates you to do. We implement the technical controls, produce the configuration evidence behind each, and report status accurately so nobody signs an attestation based on a guess.
Can you support ITAR or EAR controlled technical data?
We support the technical side of it. Determining what is export controlled, and who may access it, is your export compliance responsibility, usually with counsel or a compliance advisor. Our part is making that policy enforceable in the systems: identity-based restrictions, storage and tenant choices that keep data in permitted locations, and a usable access trail.
Do you work with contractors outside Monterey County?
Yes. On-site work is easiest across Monterey County, Santa Cruz County, San Benito County, and the South Bay, and the rest is remote by design. Identity, endpoint, Microsoft 365, logging, and backup work does not require someone standing in your server room, and we say plainly when a visit is needed.
