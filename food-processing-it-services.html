What we do for processors

A processing plant is usually three networks pretending to be one: the production floor, the office, and whatever the equipment vendors plugged in at commissioning. Most incidents we get called into start where those three meet. Here is the work, written for plant managers rather than procurement decks.

Production and plant-floor network reliability

The production floor, the office, and vendor systems each get their own segment with explicit rules between them. Scales, labelers, checkweighers, and HMI panels stay reachable from what needs them and unreachable from everything else. Cabling and wireless specified for washdown areas and metal ceilings.

ERP, inventory, and traceability system uptime

Lot codes, production records, inventory moves, and shipping documents live in one system, and the deadline is the truck. We run the servers, the backups, the integrations to scales and label printers, and the links that keep receiving, production, and shipping synchronized.

Learn more

Microsoft 365 and identity for plant and office staff

QA, maintenance, supervisors, and office staff all need different access, and shared plant-floor logins need to stop being the default. We build the tenant, the groups, conditional access, and a shared-device sign-in model that works at shift change without a password the whole building knows.

Cybersecurity for operations that cannot stop

Ransomware that halts a line is a production event, not just an IT event. EDR on every endpoint, MFA everywhere, stronger sign-in for finance and purchasing, and segmentation built so an office infection has nowhere to go when it reaches for production.

Backups and disaster recovery aligned to production schedules

Sanitation windows and shift changes are your real maintenance windows, so that is when patching, reboots, and restore tests happen. Backups are monitored, immutable where it matters, and tested against how long you can genuinely run on paper.

Vendor remote access done safely

Line-equipment vendors need to reach their own controllers, and not through a shared VPN password unchanged since install. Each vendor gets a scoped account tied to one system, a logged session, MFA, and an off switch someone at your plant controls.

Food safety and the IT that supports it

Processors carry preventive controls obligations that growers do not, and the records proving them live in software. Ghosxt runs the IT systems those programs depend on. We are not a food safety consultancy, we do not write your plan, and we do not sign off on your controls.

FSMA Preventive Controls

A processing facility generates monitoring, corrective action, and verification records continuously. Most of it sits in an ERP module, a QA database, a shared drive, or spreadsheets one person owns. We keep those locations backed up, versioned, permissioned, and retained for the window the rule expects, so nothing that matters is one deleted folder away from gone.

Traceability records and recall exercises

One step back and one step forward only works if the systems on both ends agree. We keep receiving, production, and shipping records connected, keep scanners and label printers talking to the ERP, and make sure a mock recall pulls a real answer out of the system instead of becoming a manual reconstruction at 9pm.

Audit-day readiness

Audits turn into IT problems fast: a document that will not open, a laptop that cannot reach the file share from the conference room, a record nobody can produce because the person who kept it left in March. We set up document control, access, and retention ahead of time, so the systems answer cleanly on the day someone is standing in your lobby.

Record integrity and access control

Records are only as credible as the trail behind them. Named accounts instead of shared logins, permissions that match roles, enforced retention, and logging that shows who changed what and when.

To be direct about scope: Ghosxt runs the IT systems your food safety program depends on. Your qualified individual, your consultant, and your auditor own the program itself. We own uptime, access, backups, retention, and the network underneath it.

Common IT problems we see at processing plants

Four patterns we run into repeatedly at processing operations. Details are generalized, but the shape of each one is familiar to anyone who has run a plant.

Label printers down while trucks wait at the dock

Shipping labels stop printing at 2pm and the loads on the dock cannot leave without them. The cause is usually mundane: a print server that lost a driver after a patch, a printer that picked up a new address, or a firewall rule nobody wrote down. The fix is boring on purpose: reserved addressing, a documented print path, a tested spare, and monitoring that catches a dead queue before shipping does.

The ERP server nobody has patched because it can never be rebooted

Every plant has one: the box carrying the ERP or the production database that has not been restarted in a year because there is never a good time. It falls further behind on patches every month. We map real maintenance windows against sanitation and shift changes, stage patches so they roll back cleanly, and where the server cannot go down at all, build redundancy first and patch second.

A line-equipment vendor VPN that has been open since install

A packaging line or an optical sorter gets commissioned, the vendor asks for remote access, and someone stands up a VPN account to close the project out. Years later that account still works, the password is sitting in an email thread, and nobody knows which vendor employees have it. We replace it with per-vendor accounts scoped to the one machine they support, session logging, and MFA. Remote support continues; the permanent open door does not.

Office ransomware that reached the plant because everything is flat

A click in accounting becomes a production problem when the office, the production floor, and vendor equipment all share one flat network. Nothing slows lateral movement, so an infection that should have cost one workstation costs a shift. Segmenting the plant floor away from the office is the first fix, and it is almost always cheaper than the outage it prevents.

FAQs about IT services for food processing companies

Can you work on our plant floor network without interrupting production?
Yes. We plan floor work around sanitation windows and shift changes, and stage anything risky so it can be reversed inside the same window. For cutovers that cannot happen live, we build and test the new path in parallel, then move traffic during a scheduled break instead of mid-run.
Do you support our food ERP or traceability software?
We do not resell food ERPs or traceability platforms, and we do not replace your vendor's application support. We run everything underneath: the server or cloud tenant, backups and restore tests, accounts and single sign-on, integrations to scales, scanners, and label printers, and the network that keeps receiving, production, and shipping in agreement.
Are you a food safety consultancy?
No. We do not write food safety plans, run hazard analyses, or act as your preventive controls qualified individual. Ghosxt runs the IT systems your program depends on: document control, record retention, access control, backups, and the servers and network those records live on.
How do you handle remote access for line-equipment vendors?
Each vendor gets a named account, access limited to the equipment they support, MFA, and a logged session. No shared credentials and no standing tunnel between service calls. Access can be enabled for a service window and switched off afterward.
What happens if we get hit with ransomware during a production run?
The first hour decides most of it. We contain by isolating segments rather than pulling the whole plant offline, confirm which systems are clean, and restore production-critical systems from tested backups in the order you actually ship in. The goal is to lose a shift, not a week.
