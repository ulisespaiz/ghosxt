Managed AI Governance

Your team is already using AI with your data. ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, a meeting note-taker someone invited to a call. What you do not know is which tools, which accounts, and what each one can reach inside your email and files. Managed AI governance makes that visible, locks it down, and keeps it reviewed as the tools change. It is the same job we do for the rest of your IT: see it coming, then control it.

What is the risk of employees using AI with company data?

Most of these do not need a hacker. They take one employee, one prompt, and a default setting nobody changed.

One click grants an AI app your email and files

An employee signs in to an AI tool with their work account and approves the permission prompt. The app now holds its own access to their mail, calendar, or files, and that access keeps working long after they forget the tool exists. A malicious app can ask for the same access, so the consent setting protects against attackers as well as mistakes.

Copilot finds what was overshared

Microsoft 365 Copilot answers from whatever the user can already open. Years of SharePoint and OneDrive links set to everyone in the company mean a new hire can ask for salaries, contracts, or a patient schedule and get a tidy summary.

Sensitive data in personal AI accounts

A patient chart, a client's financials, or a grower's contract pasted into a personal ChatGPT or Gemini account leaves your tenant, your retention rules, and your audit trail. Your company cannot see it, cannot enforce its deletion, and cannot verify where it went.

Without a written policy, each employee sets the rules

Rules that were never written down are hard to enforce, and answering a customer's or insurer's questionnaire about AI use becomes guesswork.

What does managed AI governance include?

Managed AI governance is the ongoing work of finding the AI tools connected to a company's Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, limiting what each one can reach, and keeping a written policy current. Seven pieces, done in this order, then kept current. Each one produces a control or a record you can check.

AI app inventory

We find the AI tools connected to your Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace tenant: the ones IT approved, the ones a user approved for themselves, and the note-takers nobody mentioned. Browser extensions and personal accounts show up where your browser or device policy reports them. You get a list with the permissions each one holds.

Consent lockdown

We turn off user self-consent, so staff can no longer grant an app access to company data with one click. New tools go through an admin review workflow, and approved tools are limited to the groups that need them where the app supports assignment.

Data exposure cleanup before rollout

Before Copilot or any tool that reads your files goes live, we find oversharing in SharePoint, OneDrive, and Drive and fix what you approve, so we narrow what each AI tool can reach to what each person should see.

Data protection rules

Where your licensing supports it, we set up sensitivity labels and data loss prevention rules for patient, client, and financial information, so the sensitive categories are marked and, in the locations and devices we configure, handled by policy instead of by memory. Coverage depends on licensing and configuration.

A written acceptable-use policy

A short policy staff can actually follow: which tools are approved, what data stays out of them, who approves a new tool, and how AI output gets checked. Our approach is aligned with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, sized for a small business rather than an enterprise program.

Rollout of approved tools

We connect the tools you decide to keep the right way: business accounts instead of personal ones, licenses assigned to the right people, access scoped, and the settings that matter turned on before anyone starts using them.

Monthly review

AI tools add features, request new permissions, and change their defaults. Every month we review new app requests, new connections to the tenant, and changes to approved tools that show up in your tenant, such as new permissions, and we tell you what moved.

How does it work?

  1. Assess

    We inventory the AI tools connected to your tenant, check what each one can reach, and, where licensing supports it, look for sensitive data sitting in places an AI tool would surface. You get the findings in writing.

  2. Lock down

    We turn off self-consent, set up the admin review workflow, revoke access for tools nobody approved once you confirm the list, and clean up the oversharing you approve before it ends up in a chat window.

  3. Roll out

    We connect the approved tools correctly, publish the acceptable-use policy, and walk your staff through what is allowed and why in plain language.

  4. Manage monthly

    We review new requests and new connections, watch for tenant-visible changes in the tools you use, and keep the policy and the controls current as the models and the vendors change.

What do we get from the assessment?

We show you which AI tools are connected to your company, what each one can reach, and what to fix first. In writing, whether or not you hire us for the rest, and it becomes the first step of the managed add-on if you do.

What do owners ask about managed AI governance?

Is it safe to let employees use ChatGPT with company data?
It depends on the account and the data. A personal ChatGPT account is outside your control: your company cannot see what was pasted, enforce its deletion, or apply its retention rules to it. A business account with the right settings, limited to approved staff and non-sensitive data, can be used with reasonable care. The safe version is a decision you make and enforce, not one each employee makes alone.
How do I control which AI apps can access our Microsoft 365?
In Microsoft Entra, turn off user consent for third-party apps and route requests through the admin consent workflow, so an employee cannot grant an app access to mail or files on their own. Then review the apps already connected to the tenant and remove anything nobody approved. Google Workspace has a comparable control for third-party app access. We set this up and handle the review of new requests as part of the managed add-on.
Should we turn on Microsoft 365 Copilot?
Not until you know what it can reach. Copilot answers from whatever each user already has permission to open, so files overshared in SharePoint and OneDrive become answers in a chat window. Clean up sharing first, add sensitivity labels with the protections that limit Copilot where your licensing supports them, then roll Copilot out to a pilot group and widen from there.
Can healthcare offices use AI tools with patient information?
Only in tools covered by a signed business associate agreement for that product and plan and configured for it, with access limited to the staff who need it. Patient data stays out of everything else, including personal accounts and consumer AI note-takers. We help you document which tools are approved for patient information and set controls to block the rest where your tenant and devices allow.
Are AI meeting note-takers safe to use at work?
Treat them like any other app with access to your calendar and your calls. Decide which note-taker is approved, who may use it, and whether it may join calls with clients or patients, then block the rest from joining where your meeting platform allows it. Many of them ask for calendar access and can be set to join every meeting automatically, so the settings matter more than the brand.
Do we need an AI policy?
Yes, and it should fit on two pages. It names the approved tools, what data can and cannot go into them, who approves a new tool, and how AI-generated output gets checked before it is used. Staff follow short, specific rules; few people follow a thirty-page document. We write it with you and keep it current as the tools change.
