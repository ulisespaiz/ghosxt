How MSPs price managed IT

Before you compare quotes, find out which pricing model each one uses. Each model puts the risk on a different side of the table, and a low headline number can hide which side you're on.

Per user. You pay one monthly price for each person, and it covers that person's support, monitoring, patching, and security. It tracks headcount, the number you already budget around, and it prices what actually gets attacked, which is people's accounts and inboxes. It's the most common model for small-business managed IT, and it's the one we use for teams of 5 or more.

Per device. You pay for each computer, server, or phone under management. The headline number often looks lower until you count the second laptop, the desktop at home, and the phone. It also prices hardware instead of identity, which is backwards now that so many breaches start with a stolen login rather than a broken machine. We use per-device pricing for infrastructure that isn't tied to one person: servers ($200 a month), firewalls ($50), managed switches ($25), wireless access points ($20), and NAS storage ($100). Enrolling a laptop or phone in device management is a $15 per device monthly add-on.

Flat rate. One monthly number for the whole company, whatever its size. It's simple to budget, but the provider has to pad it to cover what it can't see, and it gets renegotiated every time you hire. We use a flat rate in one place, where the simplicity helps everyone: Tiny Team, $600 a month for 1 to 4 users.

Hourly, or break-fix. You pay only when something breaks. It's the cheapest option in a quiet year and the most expensive in a bad one, and in between nobody is patching, monitoring, or checking your backups. The provider also earns more when things go wrong, which is the wrong incentive. We bill hourly only for work outside a managed agreement: project work at $175 an hour, on-site visits for managed clients at $150, and remote or on-site help for non-managed clients at $180 or $220. For the full comparison, see break-fix vs. managed IT.

What we use, and why. A mix, on purpose: per user for people, per device for infrastructure and enrolled laptops and phones, flat for very small teams, and hourly for projects. Every rate is published, so you can check our math against the pricing page.

What's usually not included

A managed IT fee covers keeping what you have secure, patched, backed up, and supported. It doesn't cover buying new things or building new things. Budget for these separately, whoever you hire:

  • Project work. New server builds, office moves, network redesigns, and configuration work inside your business apps. We bill project work at $175 an hour.
  • Hardware. Laptops, desktops, firewalls, switches, and access points. You own the equipment. We spec it and help you buy it, and managing network gear afterward is the per-device add-on.
  • Software licenses. Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace licenses and your line-of-business apps are paid to the vendor, not bundled into our fee.
  • After-hours and emergency work. Support runs 8am to 6pm Pacific, Monday through Friday. Compliance & Continuity includes after-hours support. On other plans, after-hours work is $250 an hour, and emergency work outside an agreement is $350 an hour.
  • On-site time. Remote help desk is part of every plan. On-site visits for managed clients are $150 an hour.
  • Server image backup. Quoted per server, because it scales with your hardware and data.

Managed IT cost: common questions

Is $150 per user a month normal for IT support?
Yes, for fully managed IT. Our own published tiers are $125, $175, and $250 per user per month, so $150 sits between the first two. The better question is what the $150 includes. If managed detection and response, backup, or security awareness training show up as separate line items, compare total monthly cost, not the headline rate.
How much does IT support cost for a 10-person office?
On our published pricing, a 10-person office pays $1,250 a month on Core Managed IT ($125 per user), $1,750 on Secure Growth ($175 per user), or $2,500 on Compliance & Continuity ($250 per user), plus $1,500 one-time onboarding on the Microsoft 365 default scope. Add $200 a month for a server and $50 for a firewall if you have them.
What does managed IT cost for a business with 2 or 3 employees?
$600 a month, flat, for 1 to 4 users on our Tiny Team plan. That includes monitoring, help desk, patching, managed detection and response, MFA, email security, cloud backup for Microsoft 365 and OneDrive, and security awareness training. One-time onboarding is $1,000.
Is managed IT cheaper than hiring an IT person?
For most small teams, yes. One in-house hire costs a full California salary plus benefits and still leaves you uncovered when that person is sick or on vacation. The calculator on our pricing page compares the two for your headcount, and for most small teams it comes out roughly 73 to 79 percent less than building the same skills in-house.
Is there a setup fee for managed IT?
Yes, a one-time onboarding fee. It's $1,000 for Tiny Team. For the per-user plans it depends on headcount: 5 to 15 users is $1,500, 15 to 30 users is $2,500, 30 to 50 users is $4,000, and above 50 is a custom quote. Those fees cover the Microsoft 365 default scope. Google Workspace and Apple fleet onboarding is scoped and quoted separately.
Do I have to sign a long-term IT contract?
Our standard agreement is 12 months, with no price increase during the term. Leaving early during that first term costs 50% of the remaining months. After 12 months you can cancel with 30 days' written notice and no fee. If you want no commitment at all, month-to-month is available from day one at 15% more.
Do you charge more for Google Workspace or Macs?
The monthly per-user price is the same. Any laptop or phone enrolled in device management adds $15 per device per month, and Google Workspace and Apple fleet onboarding is scoped and quoted separately instead of using the Microsoft 365 onboarding fee.
Do you serve Salinas, Monterey, and Santa Cruz?
Yes. We're based in Salinas and work on-site across Monterey County and in Santa Cruz, San Benito, and Santa Clara counties, and remotely anywhere in the US. The prices on this page are the same in every city.
