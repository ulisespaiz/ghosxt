Why Salinas facility networks fail

Facility networks in the Salinas Valley break for physical, traceable reasons. Consumer and light-commercial gear gets installed in buildings it was never rated for, then grows one addition at a time until nobody can say what is plugged in where. These are the failure patterns we find most often when we open up a network in a Salinas warehouse, pack house, cooler, or yard office:

  • Metal buildings and cold rooms that swallow Wi-Fi. Steel racking, refrigeration panels, and stacked pallets absorb and reflect signal, so a layout that tested fine in an empty building has dead aisles once product is in it.
  • Scanners dropping between racking aisles. Handhelds roam badly when access points were placed for coverage on paper instead of for the path a picker actually walks, and the drops always surface during a shipping window.
  • Unmanaged switches daisy-chained over years. A desktop switch under the office desk feeding another one on the dock, no port map, no VLAN capability, and no way to see which uplink is saturated or which run is failing.
  • One flat network. Office PCs, cameras, cold-chain sensors, refrigeration controllers, printers, and the WMS all share a single segment, so an infection on a front-office machine can reach every operational system behind it.
  • Aging cabling. Runs pulled years ago, terminated by whoever was available, sitting in conduit alongside motors and compressors. Intermittent link errors get blamed on software for months.
  • ISP outages with no failover. One circuit, no backup path, and a scale house or dispatch desk that stops working entirely until the carrier gets a truck out.

None of this requires a full rebuild to improve. Most of it is resolved with a real survey, a segmentation plan, a documented switch layout, and monitoring that reports problems before your crew calls them in. Ground-up architecture and deployment work is covered on our network design page. This page is about keeping a Salinas network running week after week.

What we support

Ongoing support across the whole path, from the access point in the cooler to the firewall at the edge. We are vendor-neutral and will support capable equipment you already own rather than pushing a forklift replacement.

Wi-Fi & Access Points

Coverage designed for the building you actually have: warehouse, pack house, cooler, dock, and yard. Site surveys with RF tools, antenna placement planned around metal racking, and capacity planning for seasonal density when headcount climbs during a harvest window.

Switching & VLANs

Managed switching with a documented port map, and VLAN segmentation that separates office, operations, guest, cameras, and controllers. When one device is compromised, the blast radius stops at the segment instead of reaching the file server.

Firewalls & Security

Firewall rules written to be explained, not inherited. We clean up rule sets nobody can read, close inbound ports opened years ago for a vendor who is long gone, terminate VPN properly, and keep firmware current. Endpoint and identity work lives in Salinas cybersecurity.

Site-to-Site & Remote VPN

Encrypted tunnels between a Salinas headquarters, a second yard, a cooler, and a field office, so ERP, WMS, cameras, and shared files behave the same at every location. Remote access for staff, plus scoped and time-limited access for vendors instead of a shared password and an open port.

Internet & Failover

Dual-ISP configurations and LTE backup so a cut line does not stop scanning, label printing, monitoring, or order flow. We watch circuit health, fail traffic over where the hardware supports it, and run the carrier ticket when a circuit drops.

Monitoring & Management

Alerting on switches, access points, firewalls, and circuits, with automatic configuration backups so a dead device is restored instead of rebuilt from memory. Documentation stays current: diagram, IP scheme, port maps, and credentials held in your password manager, not ours.

FAQs about network support in Salinas

Do you support networks you did not build?
Yes. Most of the networks we support in Salinas were installed by someone else, usually in pieces over many years. We start with an assessment: we log into every device we can reach and map switchports, VLANs, firewall rules, and access point placement, then give you a written current-state report. From there we stabilize and document what works and fix or replace what does not. You do not need a full redesign to get support, and nothing has to be ripped out on day one.
Can you fix Wi-Fi dead zones in a warehouse or cooler?
Usually yes, and rarely by bolting more access points to the same spots. Metal racking, concrete, refrigeration panels, and stacked product absorb and reflect signal, so coverage has to be planned around the building and the way crews and scanners move through it. We survey with RF tools, model coverage against the actual construction, place and aim antennas for the aisles rather than the conference room, and set channel plans and power levels so access points stop competing with each other. Then we retest with the handhelds your team really uses.
Do you support multi-site VPNs between yards, coolers, and offices?
Yes. Site-to-site IPsec between a Salinas headquarters, a second yard, a cooler, or a field office is standard work for us, along with remote-access VPN for staff and scoped, time-limited access for vendors who need to reach one specific system. We keep a single security policy across sites, document the routing and the IP scheme, and monitor the tunnels so a drop becomes an alert instead of a phone call from dispatch.
What does network support cost in Salinas?
Ongoing network monitoring, configuration management, firmware patching, and support are included in our managed IT plans, and those plans are published upfront: Tiny Team Managed Security, Core Managed IT, Secure Growth, and Compliance and Continuity. Project work such as a wireless survey, a switch replacement, a firewall migration, or new cabling is scoped and quoted separately. Start with the free 30-minute assessment and you get a written scope and a real number before anything is ordered.
Do you replace our internet provider?
No. We are not an ISP and we do not resell circuits. We design around the connectivity you can get at your address: dual-ISP failover where two carriers are available, LTE backup for a site with only one usable circuit, and monitoring that flags a circuit degrading before it fails outright. We also own the vendor relationship during an outage, which means we open the ticket, escalate it, and keep your team updated rather than leaving your office manager on hold.
